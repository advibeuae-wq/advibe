DHA Ad Rules 2026: What Dubai Clinics Can and Can't Say in Their Ads

Since August, DHA has banned words like "the best," "100%" and "no side effects" in medical ads on social media. Here's what changed, plus a free guide with compliant rewrites.

If your clinic's Instagram or Google ads still say "the best dermatologist in Dubai" or "100% safe, no side effects," they're no longer compliant.

Since August, the Dubai Health Authority (DHA) has been enforcing stricter rules on how medical and healthcare services are advertised on social media — and the words most clinics have used for years are exactly the ones now flagged.

This isn't a vague guideline clinics can quietly ignore. It affects what gets approved on Meta, what a compliance review flags, and ultimately what can get an ad — or an account — pulled down.

What actually changed

DHA's advertising standards for healthcare facilities and practitioners were always fairly strict on paper. What changed is enforcement, specifically around social media ads, where clinics had historically written more freely than they would in print or on a clinic sign.

The core principle hasn't moved: a medical ad has to be accurate, verifiable, and can't create unrealistic expectations about outcomes. What's different now is that absolute claims, comparative superlatives, and guarantees are being treated as clear violations rather than marketing language that slides through.

Words and claims that are now flagged

Based on what's being rejected across clinic accounts we work with, a few categories come up repeatedly:

  • Superlatives: "the best," "number 1," "top clinic in Dubai."
  • Absolute percentages: "100% effective," "100% safe," "guaranteed results."
  • Risk-free claims: "no side effects," "zero downtime," "completely painless."
  • Before/after promises without context: implying a specific, guaranteed outcome for every patient.
  • Unverified comparisons: claiming to outperform a named or implied competitor.

None of these are niche phrasing choices — they're some of the most common lines clinics have used in ad copy for years, which is exactly why this shift matters.

What you can say instead

The goal isn't to strip all personality or confidence out of a clinic's ads. It's to replace unverifiable, absolute claims with language that's accurate and still persuasive.

As a general direction: trade superlatives for specifics, trade guarantees for process, and trade "no risk" language for honest expectation-setting.

"The best skin clinic in Dubai" becomes a line about what the clinic actually does differently. "100% safe, no side effects" becomes a line about what patients are told during consultation. The claim gets smaller and more specific — and that's usually what makes it compliant.

Doing this properly across every treatment, every platform, and every ad set is a real rewrite project, not a five-minute edit. That's exactly what we put together in the free guide below — compliant rewrites for the claims we see flagged most often, mapped against what DHA is actually enforcing.

Who this applies to

This covers any DHA-licensed facility or practitioner advertising in Dubai — clinics, dental practices, aesthetic and dermatology centers, and wellness providers running paid or organic social content about their services.

It applies whether the ad is a paid Meta campaign, a boosted post, or an organic caption. Enforcement doesn't distinguish between ad spend and a regular post — the claim itself is what gets reviewed.

What's at risk if you don't adjust

Beyond the compliance side, there's a practical marketing risk: ads using flagged language are increasingly getting rejected at the platform level before they even run, which means wasted setup time and delayed campaigns.

For clinics already running paid social, it's worth auditing current ad copy now rather than finding out a campaign has been rejected mid-month.

FAQ

Does this apply to organic Instagram captions, or only paid ads?

Both. The rule is about the claim being made, not the budget behind it. An organic caption with "100% safe, no side effects" carries the same risk as a paid ad with the same line.

Can we still show before/after photos?

Generally yes, but the framing matters. Before/after photos paired with a guaranteed-outcome claim are the problem — the photo itself is usually fine when it's presented as one patient's result rather than a universal promise.

What should we do with ads that are already live?

Review and update the copy rather than waiting for a rejection or takedown. It's quicker to fix wording proactively than to rebuild an ad set after it's been flagged.

Is this guide legal advice?

No. It's a practical marketing reference based on what we're seeing enforced across clinic accounts. For a definitive compliance read on your specific services, check directly with DHA or your clinic's regulatory contact.
